Set an object's position from its code block by picking a spot on the canvas, or by lining it up with another object. Find the object's block

Click Select in the gizmo bar, then click the object you want to position. Its block is highlighted on the workspace.

Alternative: Use the arrow keys to move the cursor over the object, then press Enter.

Click the pin

Click the position pin on the object's code block. The object you are positioning is selected, with a box outline around it, so you can see which object will move.

Alternative: Move to the pin on the block with the keyboard and press Enter.

 Pick a spot

Click any surface on the canvas. The block's X, Y, and Z values update and the object moves so it rests against the surface at the spot you picked: on a floor, against a wall, or under a ceiling. Inside a room, click its floor or one of its walls.

You can drag to turn the camera while you pick; dragging doesn't change the position. To stop without changing the position, click off the canvas.

Alternative: Use the arrow keys to move the yellow cursor over a surface and press Enter. Hold Shift with the arrow keys to move the cursor in smaller steps. Press Escape to stop without changing the position.

Line up with another object

While the pin is active, alignment handles appear on the sides of objects near the camera, on the sides that face you. Objects further away don't show handles, so if the object you want isn't showing any, move the camera closer to it. Each handle places your object so it touches that side:

  • A dot in the middle of a side places your object in the middle of it.
  • A bar along an edge also lines your object up with that edge.
  • An L shape in a corner tucks your object into that corner.

The colors show which way each handle lines your object up, matching the gizmo arrows: X is blue, Y is green, and Z is orange.

Move the pointer over a handle so it turns yellow, then click. To stack one object on top of another, click the dot on top of it.

Alternative: Use the arrow keys to move the yellow cursor over a handle until it turns yellow, then press Enter.

Check the rotation

Picking a position never changes your object's rotation. If the two objects are turned differently, they touch but may not sit flat against each other. Use Rotate to turn your object first.

On small objects you may only see the dots, so move closer to see the edge and corner handles. Glide and keyframe blocks only pick a spot; they don't show alignment handles.
